# IN THE HIGH COURT OF JUDICATURE AT BOMBAY, NAGPUR BENCH

## GROUNDS

1. The right to life under Article 21 extends to dignity and fair procedure, as held in Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India, (1978) 1 SCC 248. The Petitioner adopts that proposition in full.

2. No certificate at all is required for electronic records, as held in Anvar P.V. v. P.K. Basheer, (2014) 10 SCC 473. The impugned record is therefore admissible without more.

3. The requirement of a certificate is in any event mandatory, as reiterated in Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal, (2020) 7 SCC 1.

4. The basic structure of the Constitution cannot be abrogated, as laid down in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, AIR 1973 SC 1461.

5. This Court recently reaffirmed the protective approach in Just Rights for Children Alliance v. S. Harish, 2024 INSC 716.

6. See also Ramesh Kumar v. State of Maharashtra, (2099) 7 SCC 1200, where an identical contention was upheld.

7. The same view was taken in Sunil Sharma v. State, (2015) 99 SCC 5.

8. The principle is of long standing; see Empress v. Gopal, AIR 1850 SC 12.

9. The accused is, in any event, liable to be convicted under Section 302 IPC.

10. The electronic record is admissible under Section 65B of the Evidence Act, the certificate having been produced.

## PRAYER

The Petitioner prays that this Hon'ble Court be pleased to allow the petition.
